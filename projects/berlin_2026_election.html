Berlin's 2026 Abgeordnetenhaus Election

Link to dashboard

I wanted to build an interactive dashboard to explore the results of Berlin's September 20, 2026 Abgeordnetenhaus election. The dashboard shows how the different political parties performed across Berlin and allows users to examine voting patterns at different geographic levels. In addition to the official election results, I wanted to explore how voting preferences differed across demographic groups, including age, gender, education, economic circumstances, and religious affiliation.

The processed geographic dataset contains 1,572 postal-vote-linked voting-area groups covering Berlin's 12 Bezirke. I combined the official election results with electoral-district boundaries to create an interactive map showing the distribution of votes across the city. The dataset includes results for 17 political parties, with vote counts and percentages calculated using the total number of valid votes in each area. The displayed geographic areas are processed and aggregated from the original electoral-district data.

The dashboard allows users to switch between the 12 Bezirke and the individual voting areas. They can select a political party to see its geographic distribution or display the leading party in each area. Clicking a Bezirk or voting area updates the results table, while hovering over the map displays the relevant election results. The map also includes the boundaries and names of all 12 Bezirke.

I also wanted to examine how different groups of voters supported the political parties. For this part of the project, I collected and processed published election survey estimates from ARD/infratest dimap and the Konrad-Adenauer-Stiftung election analysis, which reports findings from infratest dimap and Forschungsgruppe Wahlen. The resulting dataset contains 197 estimates across six demographic dimensions: age, gender, age combined with gender, education, self-assessed economic situation, and religion. Users can select two groups and compare their estimated party vote shares using interactive charts.

One important distinction is that the geographic election results and demographic voting estimates come from different types of data. The map is based on recorded votes aggregated into geographic areas, whereas the demographic comparisons are based on Berlin-wide election surveys. These survey estimates cannot be used to determine how particular demographic groups voted within an individual Bezirk or voting area. They are also subject to sampling uncertainty and differences in the categories reported by each source.

I prepared and validated the datasets using Python and Jupyter Notebook, including checks of electoral identifiers, geographic assignments, vote totals, and party percentages. The geographic boundaries were processed and converted into a format suitable for displaying the results in a web browser. I also combined the demographic survey data into a consistent structure for comparison in the dashboard.

I developed the dashboard with the assistance of ChatGPT, using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. The interactive map uses SVG rather than an external mapping library or background map tiles. The election and survey data are loaded automatically from preprocessed files, so the dashboard does not require a Python backend or database. It can be hosted as a static website.

The project is intended as an exploratory visualization of Berlin's 2026 election results. It provides a way to compare parties across different parts of the city and examine broader demographic voting patterns. However, geographic differences should not be interpreted as evidence of how individual residents voted, and the survey estimates should be understood within the limitations of their respective sources.

Data sources

  • Amt für Statistik Berlin-Brandenburg — 2026 election results : official results and downloadable voting-district spreadsheets.
  • Berlin Open Data — electoral-district geometries (2026) : geographic boundary files and dataset metadata.
  • ARD / Tagesschau — vote shares by age : published election-survey charts from infratest dimap.
  • ARD / Tagesschau — vote shares by gender : published election-survey charts from infratest dimap.
  • Konrad-Adenauer-Stiftung — Berlin election analysis (PDF download) : source tables for age by gender, education, self-assessed economic situation, and religion, including figures from infratest dimap and Forschungsgruppe Wahlen.

The election results and boundary datasets are available to download. The survey links provide published graphics or aggregated tables, not individual respondents' microdata. The demographic estimates are Berlin-wide, should not be interpreted as Bezirk-level measurements, and do not establish causal relationships. Reported categories differ across the underlying surveys.
